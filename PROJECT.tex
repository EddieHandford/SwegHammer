\section{Project vision}

SwegHammer is a data-driven Warhammer 40K simulator with two goals:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item Derive fair, transparent points costs from Lanchester's Square
    Law plus empirical simulation.
  \item Test minimal rule tweaks (unit-by-unit activation,
    command-point compensation for the smaller army) that fix structural
    feel-bad moments like first-player advantage.
\end{enumerate}

The simulator is the experimental apparatus. It must be \emph{fast}
enough to run thousands of battles in a calibration sweep, and
\emph{detailed} enough to render a single battle as a watchable replay
for sanity-checking the model.

\paragraph{Pipeline structure.} The project runs as two sequenced
feedback loops, not one. Stage 1 (Goal A below) tunes the simulator's
rules until per-faction win rates match the tournament aggregate;
Stage 2 (Goal C below) freezes the rules and tunes unit points costs
until win rates across the catalogue flatten. The figure below shows
the relationship; \texttt{OVERVIEW.tex} is the standalone shareable
version with non-technical wording.

\section{The four goals}
\label{sec:goals}

As of the 2026-05 reorganisation, project work is tracked against four
goals rather than the previous phase numbering. The phases that delivered
the substrate (Phase 0 / 1 / 1.5 / 1.6, Phase A2 / B / C / D / E) are
shipped and live in \texttt{ROADMAP.md} as a historical record. Active
work tracks against the goals below.

\subsection*{Goal A --- Sim matches real tournament data}
Drive the per-faction matchup-matrix MAE down by implementing the
faction-specific mechanics that distinguish factions in real play.
Headline metric: MAE vs the May 2026 Warp Friends tournament aggregate,
currently $5.48$ pts at $N{=}30$ / $7.01$ pts at $N{=}200$ (the honest
reading), target $\le 2.0$ pts. As of 2026-05-15, 9 of 10 faction army
rules ship (Tyranids Synapse, Drukhari Pain Tokens, AdMech Doctrina,
GSC Cult Ambush, World Eaters Blood Tithe, Death Guard Disgustingly
Resilient $+$ Contagions, Thousand Sons All Is Dust, Necrons Reanimation,
Orks WAAAGH! $+$ Mob Rule). Marines Oath of Moment remains
\texttt{[NEEDS RETRY]}. Plus 5 core 10e rules shipped this session:
Look Out Sir, Lone Operative, Deadly Demise X, Fall Back, Desperate
Escape. See \texttt{scripts/evaluate\_vs\_meta.py}.

\subsection*{Goal B --- Equal-quality simulation per faction}
Bring every faction to the same depth of in-game representation. As of
2026-05-15: 25 detachments registered (up from 21), 5 new per-faction
stratagems on top of the 4 universal core $+$ 3 original detachment
sets, 5 Warlord enhancements, 4 CP-discount HQs, 32 leader abilities
all with verbatim Wahapedia citations, $122/122$ rules cited. A
mechanic-fire validator (\texttt{tests/test\_faction\_mechanic\_smoke.py})
confirms 19 implemented faction mechanics actually trigger in seeded
battles.

\subsection*{Goal C --- Generate balanced SwegHammer points (two-track)}
Replace GW's commercially-driven points with empirically-derived costs.
Two independent solvers run in parallel; the divergence between them is
itself a calibration signal:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Sweg-balancer} (\texttt{code/balancer.py}) ---
    Monte Carlo bisection. For each unit, bisect points-per-model until
    armies-of-U vs armies-of-baseline-peer land at $50\% \pm 5\%$. Slow,
    but exercises \emph{every} rule in the simulator (movement, range,
    terrain, charge variance, objective contest, CP).
  \item \textbf{Equilibrium} (\texttt{code/equilibrium.py}, Ed's solver)
    --- closed-form Bradley-Terry / log-LSQ on the pairwise time-to-kill
    matrix. All 6 phases shipped: 1 (shoot), 2 ($+$melee), 3 (defensive
    audit), 4 (tactical utility), 5 (meta-weighted), 6 (Nash mixed-
    strategy).
\end{itemize}

A cross-validation script (\texttt{scripts/cross\_validate\_pricing.py})
compares the two signals; 2 disagreements (Wraithguard, Crisis Sunforge)
and 5 confirmations on the live catalogue. Both tracks are blocked on
Ed's points-per-model import fix --- until \texttt{points\_cost}
returns the GW per-model cost rather than the Lanchester-derived score,
the sim's army budgets run in wrong-currency.

\subsection*{Goal D --- Price non-damaging abilities}
Speed, deep strike, scout, sticky objective, OC bonuses, re-deploy ---
abilities that don't show up in the pairwise damage matrix but are real
points value. Implemented as Equilibrium Phase 4's
\texttt{tactical\_value(u)} multiplicative log-overlay on Phase 2.
Weights grid-search calibrated on 9 anchors; the anchor set under-
identifies \texttt{w\_move}, \texttt{w\_oc}, \texttt{w\_infiltrator},
\texttt{w\_deep\_strike} (all calibrate to 0). Widening the anchor set
is the next Goal D step.

\section{Sprint plan: May 2026 handover (Ed/Jake)}
\label{sec:sprint-may-2026}

This section captures the architecture clarification and near-term work
sequence agreed between Ed and Jake on 2026-05-15. It supersedes any
ad-hoc next-steps notes scattered through the older sections.

\subsection*{Architecture clarification}

SwegHammer is two interlocking systems, not one:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Equilibrium model} (\texttt{code/equilibrium.py}) ---
    the core mathematics-hammer engine. Lancaster-derived,
    multi-dimensional, non-linear. Computes pairwise unit matchup
    outcomes in closed form. \emph{This is what ships in the final
    product.}
  \item \textbf{Simulator} (\texttt{code/simulator.py}) --- exists to
    tune the utility factors (deep strike, sticky objectives, scout,
    re-deploy and so on) that feed back into the equilibrium model.
    Slow and iterative; only needed for calibration.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why the split matters.} The end-user-facing front end
(``Fire Prism vs Wraithknight --- what are the odds?'') runs the
equilibrium equation only. The simulator stays in the toolbox for the
specific case where Games Workshop releases a weird new model and we
need to derive its tactical utility weights before plugging it into
the equation.

\paragraph{Calibration constraint.} Real tournament data is scarce.
Even a major event like the Las Vegas Open produces only a few
thousand games across roughly twenty factions \textemdash{} too thin
for faction-vs-faction matchup matrices on its own. The simulator
covers the gap by generating the synthetic matchup volume the
analytic solver needs.

\subsection*{Current focus (Jake)}

Drive the simulator's mean absolute error down to 2--4\,\% against
real tournament results, using the Games Workshop printed point costs
as the baseline for army budgets. The current honest reading is
$7.01$ points at $N{=}200$ (see \S\ref{sec:goals} Goal A); 2--4\,\%
is the threshold at which the simulator's outputs are trustworthy
enough to drive points re-derivation in production.

  \item \wip \jake\ Get the simulator's mean absolute error down to

  \item \todo \either\ Generate a trial balanced points dataset.

\section{Game phases}
\label{sec:phases}

40K splits a turn into discrete phases. We will mirror that structure
but keep each phase pluggable so we can disable expensive ones for fast
calibration runs.

\subsection{Command phase}
At the start of a unit's activation: gain 1 CP, resolve start-of-turn
abilities, apply the smaller-army CP compensation rule.

\paragraph{To do}
\begin{itemize}[label={},leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \todo \eddie\ Add a \texttt{CommandPhase} step run at activation
    start.
  \item \todo \eddie\ Move existing CP bonus logic out of
    \texttt{Battle.\_run\_round} into the new phase so it lives in one
    place.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Movement phase}
A unit moves up to its Move characteristic in inches. Optionally Advance
($+d6$, no shooting that turn) or Fall Back (no shooting, no charging).

\paragraph{Math.} Position update for a unit at $\mathbf{p}_t$ choosing
destination $\mathbf{p}_{t+1}$ subject to
\[
  \lVert \mathbf{p}_{t+1} - \mathbf{p}_t \rVert
  \;\leq\; M_{\text{unit}} \cdot \sigma_{\text{terrain}},
\]
where $\sigma_{\text{terrain}} \in (0,1]$ is the terrain-slowdown factor
on the line the unit traverses.

\paragraph{To do}
\begin{itemize}[label={},leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \done \eddie\ Add \texttt{position: (float, float)} to
    \texttt{Unit}.
  \item \done \eddie\ Add \texttt{move: float} (inches) to
    \texttt{UnitProfile}.
  \item \done \eddie\ Implement \texttt{MovementPhase} -- move-then-shoot
    sub-phase pair per activation. Targeting is now objective-aware via
    \texttt{code/strategy.py} (HOLD / CAPTURE / STEAL / ENGAGE /
    REPOSITION intents); units sitting on objectives stay put rather
    than abandoning VP to chase the nearest enemy.
  \item \done \either\ Advance roll ($+d6$, no shooting that turn)
    implemented; range threshold is weapon range when engaging and
    objective control radius when capturing. Fall Back still
    unimplemented.
  \item \todo \either\ Segment-vs-impassable-terrain collision (only
    the destination is currently checked, so units can tunnel through
    walls).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Shooting phase}
For every weapon in range and with line of sight: roll to hit, roll to
wound, target rolls armour save, apply damage. \textbf{The current sim
skips the wound roll}, which is the biggest single bit of math debt.

\paragraph{Math.} Expected damage of one shot:
\[
  \mathbb{E}[D] \;=\; P_{\text{hit}}
  \cdot P_w(S, T)
  \cdot \bigl(1 - P_{\text{save}}(\mathrm{sv}, \mathrm{AP}, \mathrm{cover})\bigr)
  \cdot d,
\]
where the wound probability $P_w$ follows the strength-vs-toughness
table:
\[
  P_w(S, T) =
  \begin{cases}
    5/6 & S \geq 2T \\
    4/6 & S > T \\
    3/6 & S = T \\
    2/6 & S < T \\
    1/6 & 2S \leq T
  \end{cases}
\]
and the save probability uses an effective save characteristic
$\mathrm{sv}^* = \mathrm{sv} - \mathrm{AP} - \mathrm{cover}$ capped at
$2$:
\[
  P_{\text{save}}(\mathrm{sv}^*) \;=\;
  \max\!\Bigl(0,\; \tfrac{7 - \mathrm{sv}^*}{6}\Bigr).
\]

\paragraph{To do}
\begin{itemize}[label={},leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \done \eddie\ Add \texttt{strength: int} to \texttt{UnitProfile}.
  \item \done \eddie\ Add \texttt{toughness: int} to \texttt{UnitProfile}.
  \item \done \eddie\ Implement the wound table above as
    \texttt{wound\_probability(S, T)} and wire it into
    \texttt{Unit.attack()} between the hit and save rolls.
  \item \todo \eddie\ Regenerate \texttt{BASELINE.md} catalogue table to
    reflect the wound-roll-aware point costs (existing table is now
    stale; mirror match holds on symmetric terrain, but cross-faction
    matchups need recalibrating).
  \item \done \eddie\ Add \texttt{range\_inches: int} to the weapon
    profile; gate shooting on range and LoS. Liang-Barsky
    segment-vs-rectangle intersection handles obscuring terrain.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Charge phase}
Declare a charge against an enemy within $12''$. Roll $2d6$; if the
total is at least the distance to the target, move into base contact.

\paragraph{Math.}
\[
  P_{\text{charge}}(d) \;=\; \Pr[2d_6 \geq d]
  \;=\; \frac{\#\{(a,b) \in [1,6]^2 : a+b \geq d\}}{36}.
\]
Useful values: $P(6'') = 26/36 \approx 72\%$,
$P(9'') = 10/36 \approx 28\%$, $P(12'') = 1/36 \approx 3\%$.

\paragraph{To do}
\begin{itemize}[label={},leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \done \either\ Implement \texttt{ChargePhase} with declaration,
    $2d6$ roll and move-into-engagement ($1''$ of target). Lives in
    \texttt{Battle.\_do\_charge}; gated by a charge-desire heuristic
    so pure shooters don't suicide-charge out of range.
  \item \done \either\ \texttt{\_charging\_this\_round} set tracked on
    \texttt{Battle}; chargers fight first in the melee phase.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Fight (melee) phase}
Units in base contact fight. Each model makes \texttt{attacks} attacks
at \texttt{weapon\_skill}, then wound, save, damage as per shooting.

\paragraph{To do}
\begin{itemize}[label={},leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Morale / battleshock phase}
A unit that lost models tests Leadership; a failed test inflicts further
casualties or status effects.

\section{Terrain and map}
\label{sec:terrain}

\subsection{Coordinate system}
Continuous 2D plane, units in inches. Standard boards: $44'' \times
60''$ (Combat Patrol), $44'' \times 90''$ (Strike Force). Models are
circles with a defined base radius.

\subsection{Terrain types}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Type & Effect & Notes \\
\midrule
Open        & none                              & default \\
Light cover & $+1$ save vs ranged               & matches today's ``in cover'' flag \\
Heavy cover & $+1$ save and $-1$ to hit         & ruins, barricades \\
Obscuring   & blocks LoS through it             & forests, walls \\
Impassable  & blocks movement                   & cliffs \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\subsection{Line of sight}
Naive implementation: draw a segment from the attacker centre to the
target centre, check intersection with any obscuring polygon. If that
becomes a perf bottleneck, swap in a spatial hash over terrain
polygons.

\section{Visualization: the dual-mode plan}
\label{sec:viz}

The simulator needs to do two things that pull in opposite directions:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item Run thousands of fast battles for calibration sweeps.
  \item Render a single battle in detail for sanity checks and demos.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Design.} Keep the simulation core
(\texttt{simulator.py}, \texttt{army.py}, \texttt{units.py})
\emph{completely headless and deterministic given a seed}. Build
rendering on top as an event-subscriber layer:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{Detailed mode} -- a \emph{Pygame} or
    \emph{matplotlib-animation} renderer with units drawn as tokens on
    a 2D top-down map, with range circles, LoS lines, charge arcs. Used
    for: replaying a single battle, demo videos, debugging movement bugs.
  \item \textbf{Fast mode} -- \emph{no rendering at all}, just numerical
    state. Used for: Monte Carlo sweeps where we run $10^3$--$10^4$
    battles per matchup and only care about win rates and attrition
    curves.
\end{itemize}

Crucially, the two modes share \emph{the same} \texttt{Battle} object;
the mode flag just toggles which subscriber is attached. A bug in
the visualisation therefore cannot affect calibration numbers.

\paragraph{To do}
\begin{itemize}[label={},leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \done \eddie\ Refactor \texttt{Battle.run()} to emit events
    via a subscriber list. Events defined in \texttt{code/events.py}:
    \texttt{BattleStarted}, \texttt{RoundStarted}, \texttt{UnitActivated},
    \texttt{UnitMoved}, \texttt{UnitShot}, \texttt{UnitKilled},
    \texttt{RoundEnded}, \texttt{BattleEnded}.
  \item \done \eddie\ Null-subscriber fast mode -- empty subscriber list
    means zero per-event overhead in calibration sweeps.
  \item \todo \either\ \texttt{PygameRenderer} -- consumes the event
    stream, paints frames live.
  \item \done \eddie\ Matplotlib static-frame renderer
    (\texttt{code/renderer.py}) reconstructing world state from the
    event stream; feeds the Streamlit replay slider page. Renders
    advance / charge / melee / objective events with directional
    arrows, star bursts on hits, and gold rings around scoring
    objective markers.
\end{itemize}

\section{Combat math reference card}

\subsection{Hit / wound / save chain}
For one attack with weapon profile $(P_{\text{hit}}, S, \mathrm{AP}, d)$
against target $(T, \mathrm{sv})$ and cover bonus $c \in \{0,1\}$:
\[
  \mathbb{E}[\text{damage}]
  \;=\; P_{\text{hit}}
  \cdot P_w(S, T)
  \cdot \bigl(1 - P_{\text{save}}(\mathrm{sv} - \mathrm{AP} - c)\bigr)
  \cdot d.
\]

\subsection{Lanchester score}
\[
  \text{Score}(\text{unit}) \;=\; \bigl(H \cdot D \cdot P_{\text{hit}}\bigr)^2,
  \qquad
  \text{Score}(\text{army}) \;=\; \sum_i e_i^{\,2}.
\]

\subsection{Charge probability}
\[
  P_{\text{charge}}(d)
  \;=\; \frac{1}{36}
    \sum_{a=1}^{6} \sum_{b=1}^{6} \mathbf{1}\!\left[a+b \geq d\right].
\]

\subsection{Primary VP scoring and win condition}
At end of each round, for every objective $o$ on the map let
$\mathrm{OC}^A_o, \mathrm{OC}^B_o$ be the total Objective Control of
each side's alive units inside the objective's control radius
(Battleshocked units contribute $\mathrm{OC} = 0$). The side with the
strict majority banks $o.\text{vp\_per\_round}$ Primary VP that round.
\[
  \mathrm{VP}^A \mathrel{+}= \sum_{o \in O}
    o.v \cdot \mathbf{1}\!\left[\mathrm{OC}^A_o > \mathrm{OC}^B_o\right],
  \qquad
  \mathrm{VP}^B \text{ symmetrically}.
\]
Win condition (replaces ``most surviving units''):
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item One-sided wipe: if only one side has alive units, that side wins.
  \item Else: higher Primary VP wins.
  \item VP tie: higher remaining army points wins ($10\%$ margin else
    draw).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Strategy layer}
Movement targeting is no longer ``march at the nearest enemy''. The
strategy layer (\texttt{code/strategy.py}) selects a $(\text{target\_pos},
\text{intent})$ per activation from five intents: \textsc{Hold},
\textsc{Capture}, \textsc{Steal}, \textsc{Engage}, \textsc{Reposition}.
Objectives are scored by current control state (enemy-held $\times 3.5$
to bias steals, uncontested $\times 2.5$, ours $\times 1.0$) and
distance-weighted; role bias (\texttt{code/roles.py}, classifying units
into \textsc{Shooty} / \textsc{Melee} / \textsc{Dual} / \textsc{Horde}
/ \textsc{Heavy} / \textsc{Support}) tips ties towards holding when in
range vs always-closing.

\section{Two-track points calibration}
\label{sec:two-track}

Goal C runs two independent points solvers. They disagree on purpose;
the disagreement is the calibration signal.

\subsection{Track 1 --- Sweg-balancer (Monte Carlo bisection)}
\texttt{code/balancer.py}; output \texttt{data/calibrated\_points.json}.

For each candidate unit $U$:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item Build two equal-points armies: one of $U$, one of a role-matched
    baseline peer (SHOOTY $\to$ Intercessor, MELEE $\to$ Assault
    Intercessor, HORDE $\to$ Boyz, HEAVY $\to$ Knight, SUPPORT $\to$
    Terminator Captain).
  \item Simulate $N$ full battles on \texttt{combat\_patrol}, with
    detachments wired on both sides.
  \item Bisect $U$'s points-per-model until the empirical win rate lands
    inside $[45\%, 55\%]$.
\end{enumerate}

Three modes:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Homogeneous} (default) --- same-role peer.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{--leader-attached}} --- for CHARACTER units, the
    candidate is built as (host $+$ leader) pairs vs (host alone) at
    equal points. Exercises the leader's aura on a real bodyguard.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{--aura-uplift}} --- for SUPPORT characters whose
    value lives entirely in buffing. Measures the win-rate delta between
    (client $+$ support) vs (client alone) and converts to points via a
    fixed linear factor.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Strengths.} The bisection sees terrain, charge variance, CP, and
objective contest. Coupling between rules is preserved.

\emph{Weaknesses.} A unit that beats average opponents but loses
catastrophically to one matchup is priced \emph{too cheaply} by the
bisection (the bad matchup is averaged away in the win-rate). It also
takes 1--10 minutes per unit and ${\sim}24$ hours for the full catalogue.

\subsection{Track 2 --- Equilibrium (closed-form log-LSQ)}
\texttt{code/equilibrium.py}; output \texttt{data/equilibrium\_points.json}
and \texttt{data/equilibrium\_points\_phase2.json}.

The premise: unit value is a function over the space of enemies it
faces. The honest answer to ``what should this unit cost?'' is the
solution of a symmetric zero-sum game over the catalogue.

For every pair $(i, j)$ of units, compute the analytic per-model damage
$D[i, j]$ inflicted by one $i$ on one $j$ per turn (hit / wound /
save / FNP / Lethal / Sustained / Twin-Linked / Devastating / Anti-X).
Then the time-to-kill matrix
\[
  T[i, j] \;=\; \frac{\text{wounds}(j)}{D[i, j]}
\]
and the log-advantage matrix
\[
  R[i, j] \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2} \log\!\left( \frac{T[j, i]}{T[i, j]} \right).
\]
The factor $\tfrac{1}{2}$ and the direction $T[j, i] / T[i, j]$ come
from the mutual-destruction equilibrium: fielding $p_j$ worth of $i$ vs
$p_j$ worth of $j$, the side that kills first should be the cheaper-per-model
unit. Solving $(p_i / p_j)^2 = T[j, i] / T[i, j]$ gives $R$ above.

The fair-trade condition $\log p_i - \log p_j \approx R[i, j]$ is then
solved in closed form by the row mean over valid entries:
\[
  \log p_i \;=\; \frac{1}{|V_i|} \sum_{j \in V_i} R[i, j] \;+\; \text{anchor shift},
\]
where $V_i$ is the set of $j$ such that both $T[i, j]$ and $T[j, i]$
are finite. One unit (Intercessor Squad at 16 pts/model) is pinned to
fix the overall scale.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item \done\ \textbf{Phase 1} --- shooting only, with the keyword

  \item \done\ \textbf{Phase 2} --- shoot $+$ melee. Per-attacker
    role-weighted mix (SHOOTY $0.90$, HEAVY $0.80$, DUAL $0.55$,

  \item \done\ \textbf{Phase 4} --- tactical-utility term

\emph{Strengths.} Fast (analytic, no simulation). Exposes the pairwise
structure --- best/worst matchups for every unit are directly readable
off $R$.

\emph{Weaknesses.} Distance-conditional keywords (Rapid Fire, Melta,
Heavy, Lance) and battlefield context (terrain, charge variance,
objective pressure) are deferred to later phases. Phase 4 is where the
analytic and empirical tracks finally have to agree.

\subsection{Why two tracks}

The bisection captures emergent dynamics the analytic solver can't see.
The equilibrium captures pairwise rock-paper-scissors structure the
bisection averages away. The \emph{divergence} between the two prices
for the same unit is more informative than either price alone:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item Balancer $\approx$ Equilibrium $\implies$ both methods agree;
    high confidence in the price.
  \item Balancer $\ll$ Equilibrium $\implies$ unit has good pairwise
    stats but loses to battlefield context (slow speed, no objective
    play, hard counter in the sim's terrain).
  \item Balancer $\gg$ Equilibrium $\implies$ unit gets value from
    non-damaging utility the analytic model doesn't see yet (this is
    the Goal D / Phase 4 signal).
\end{itemize}

\section{Rule-citation system}
\label{sec:citations}

Every 10e rule the simulator implements --- detachment flag, leader
ability, stratagem effect, simulator-side gate (Mob Rule, WAAAGH!,
Shadow in the Warp, etc.) --- carries a verbatim Wahapedia citation.
This is enforced by CLAUDE.md \S 10 and the \texttt{scripts/audit\_rules.py}
script.

\subsection{Why}
The simulator's behaviour must be auditable against canonical rules
text. Without citations the model drifts: a flag named
\texttt{bonus\_to\_hit\_when\_led} can quietly start to mean ``$+1$ to
hit'' instead of the correct ``re-roll Hit rolls of 1'', and the next
calibration sweep reports false MAE improvements driven by an invented
rule. This actually happened during the May 2026 Awakened Dynasty work,
which is the failure mode the policy exists to prevent.

\subsection{Format}
Each citation lives in \texttt{data/rule\_citations.d/*.json} (sharded
by category: detachments, leaders, stratagems, judgement\_tokens,
keywords\_and\_mechanics) or in the top-level \texttt{data/rule\_citations.json}.
Required fields per entry:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item \texttt{source} --- Wahapedia URL, or, if not on Wahapedia, the
    canonical free reference (Goonhammer datasheet readout, GW errata PDF).
  \item \texttt{rule\_name} --- exact in-game name (e.g.\ ``Reanimation
    Protocols'', ``Mob Rule'').
  \item \texttt{quoted\_text} --- verbatim rules text, including the
    ``While~\dots'' trigger half.
  \item \texttt{trigger} --- when the rule fires, in plain English.
  \item \texttt{effect} --- what changes, including ``(approximation)''
    if the simulator's behaviour diverges from canonical.
  \item \texttt{scope} --- one of \texttt{army-wide}, \texttt{unit-led},
    \texttt{character-only}, \texttt{phase-gated}, \texttt{keyword-gated},
    \texttt{weapon-keyword}.
\end{itemize}

Key format: \texttt{<REGISTRY\_NAME>.<field>} for detachment / leader /
stratagem flags, or a dotted path for simulator gates
(e.g.\ \texttt{simulator.big\_guns\_never\_tire}).

\subsection{Enforcement}
\texttt{scripts/audit\_rules.py} walks every \texttt{Detachment},
\texttt{LeaderAbility}, registered \texttt{Stratagem}, and the
hand-maintained \texttt{SIMULATOR\_RULE\_KEYS} list. For each
rule-bearing flag set away from its default, it confirms a citation
exists with all six required fields. Exit code 1 if any citation is
missing, incomplete, or stale.

Run before committing any change to \texttt{code/detachments.py},
\texttt{code/leaders.py}, \texttt{code/stratagems.py}, or rule-bearing
parts of \texttt{code/simulator.py} / \texttt{code/units.py}:
\begin{verbatim}
PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8 python -m scripts.audit_rules
\end{verbatim}

Current coverage: $95/95$ active rules cited. If you can't find a
canonical citation for a rule, \textbf{stop and ask} rather than
approximate.

\section{Matchup-matrix calibration (\texttt{evaluate\_vs\_meta})}
\label{sec:evaluate-vs-meta}

The headline calibration metric for Goal A is the mean absolute error
(MAE) between the simulator's per-faction win rate and the real
tournament aggregate. Lives in \texttt{scripts/evaluate\_vs\_meta.py}.

\subsection{How it runs}
For each ordered pair $(a, b)$ of factions in the 10-faction list, build
$N$ random $1000$-pt armies per faction and run a battle on
\texttt{DEFAULT\_MAP}. Average $A$-wins to get $\mathrm{wr}_{\text{sim}}(a, b)$.
Per-faction sim win rate is the mean of $\mathrm{wr}_{\text{sim}}(a, \cdot)$
over all opponents.

\subsection{Reference data}
The tournament-side numbers come from the Warp Friends weekly aggregate
of \emph{${\sim}10k$ games} in May 2026:

\begin{tabular}{lr}

Faction & Real WR \\
\midrule

T'au Empire      & $54.5\%$ \\
Thousand Sons    & $54.6\%$ \\
Aeldari          & $44.4\%$ \\
Orks             & $44.9\%$ \\
Leagues of Votann & $46.0\%$ (approx) \\
Adeptus Astartes & $48.0\%$ (approx) \\

\textbf{Blocker.} The Lanchester-derived \texttt{points\_cost} property

per-battle RNG is seeded by faction-index pair plus battle counter, so
the same code base produces the same matrix across re-runs --- otherwise
dice noise alone moves MAE by ${\sim}3$ pts between back-to-back
invocations.
